Proving your scenario set is diverse

The three-layer diversity report reviewers expect — and the one mistake that makes every number meaningless.

Mission tie The datasheet already commits to Vendi Score rows for the seed pool and released set. A reviewer's real question is broader: "how do I know these aren't 500 rephrasings of one customer-support ticket?" This lesson gives you the standard battery that answers it, and the vocabulary to defend each row.

One idea: diversity is three different claims

"Our scenarios are diverse" bundles three claims that need three different instruments. Tevet & Berant (2021) showed that form diversity (varied wording) and content diversity (varied meaning) are separate axes — a metric built for one is blind to the other. Your definition ("domains, roles, tone") adds a third axis that neither captures: facet coverage, meaning the spread over labeled categories.

LayerClaim it supportsStandard metrics
1. Facet coverage
your definition		"We span many domains, user roles, assistant roles, tones."Distribution table per facet + normalized Shannon entropy (0 = one category, 1 = uniform). Label facets with a judge model; human spot-check a sample and report agreement (Cohen's κ).
2. Lexical (form)"The texts aren't reworded copies of each other."Compression ratio (gzip the corpus; high ratio = repetitive), Self-BLEU (BLEU of each item against the rest; high = similar), distinct-n (unique n-grams ÷ total n-grams).
3. Semantic (content)"The situations themselves differ, not just the wording."Embed each scenario, then: mean pairwise cosine similarity, Vendi Score, and near-duplicate rate (share of items whose nearest neighbor exceeds a cosine threshold, e.g. 0.90).

Vendi Score in one sentence: it converts a similarity matrix into an effective number of distinct items — a 500-item set with Vendi 12 behaves like 12 truly different scenarios, which is the number a reviewer can feel.

The one mistake A raw diversity number is uninterpretable. Self-BLEU 0.31 — good? bad? There is no absolute scale. Every metric row needs a comparison column: a human-written reference corpus, an existing benchmark's scenario set, or your own seed pool — computed at matched sample size and similar length, with the embedding model, n-gram order, and thresholds named. Without the baseline column the table is decoration.

Known traps (reviewers check these)

  • Length confound. Distinct-n falls as texts get longer (tokens repeat), so comparing sets with different average lengths is invalid without correction (arXiv:2507.15092). Report average length next to any lexical metric.
  • Sample-size confound. Self-BLEU and Vendi both shift with N. Compare at matched N (subsample the larger set) and bootstrap a confidence interval.
  • Redundant metrics. Many scores measure the same thing. Shaib et al. found a low-correlation minimal set: compression ratio + self-repetition + Self-BLEU + one embedding-based score. More rows than that adds column inches, not evidence.
  • No dedup audit. A near-duplicate rate of ~0% after an explicit dedup pass is one line and preempts the most common objection.

Check yourself

1. The paper reports only Self-BLEU, and it is low (good). What can a reviewer still claim?

Self-BLEU is a form metric — it sees wording, not meaning or facets. Five hundred lexically distinct customer-support tickets score well. That is why layer 1 (facet coverage) and layer 3 (semantic) must be reported separately.

2. Your 500-scenario set has an embedding Vendi Score of 12. What does that mean?

Vendi is an effective count — exponential entropy of the similarity matrix's eigenvalues. 12 out of 500 signals severe mode collapse. It is not a percentage and not a literal cluster count.

3. Set A's scenarios average 900 words; the baseline corpus averages 200. Which comparison is invalid without correction?

Distinct-n is mechanically length-sensitive: the longer the text, the more n-grams repeat, so the score drops regardless of true diversity. Match lengths, truncate, or report length alongside — or the baseline column lies.

Go deeper

Primary source: Shaib et al., "Standardizing the Measurement of Text Diversity" (arXiv:2403.00553) — the one paper to read; it compares the scores empirically and ships the tool. Second read: Tevet & Berant for the form/content distinction, and DCScore (arXiv:2502.08512) if a reviewer asks for a 2025-era LLM-specific metric.
